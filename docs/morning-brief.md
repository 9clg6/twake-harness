# Morning brief

With `BRIEF_ENABLED`, the worker role wakes each owner's assistant for their morning brief, on the days and at the time they chose, Monday to Friday at 8:00 unless they chose others (see below). Every minute, it looks at each owner whose assistant is in its room, one after the other, on the wall clock of their calendar's zone, the deployment's (`ASSISTANT_TIMEZONE`) until a read named one (see Time zone), so that their brief keeps its time on that wall clock when the clocks change: on a day of their brief, from its time, it wakes their assistant through the wake-ups the events use, under the source `schedule`, the type `com.twake.harness.brief.v1`, and an id of each owner's date, `brief-<date>-` and 16 hex digits of a SHA-256 of the owner and the date, which the gateway's audit records carry as the correlation id of the brief's calls. A brief counts among the owner's `WAKEUPS_PER_HOUR`, and goes out once for each date, whether a pass runs again after a restart or on another replica; one their hourly cap held back is tried again at each pass, and logged `event capped` at the first of them alone, once in each worker process. A pass late by less than three hours still sends it; three hours past its time, a brief that never went is skipped, and the line `morning brief skipped`, once in each worker process for each time the owner chose that day, gives the owner, the date and the zone. A brief due after 21:00 has until midnight alone: one that never went by then goes no more, without that line. Another time they choose that day opens the day again from it, as a restart of the worker would; a brief that went never goes twice. The wake-ups kept are what holds a brief to once a date, so the harness refuses to start with `BRIEF_ENABLED` and a `WAKEUPS_RETENTION_MS` under two days. Only the scheduler's wake-up asks for a brief: an event published under the source `schedule` wakes nobody, and one of another source is an event's turn, whatever its type. With `BRIEF_ENABLED` off, the worker role's line `morning briefs off` says so once at its start, and its passes wake each owner's assistant for what their quiet hours held alone (see Quiet hours). The chart renders `BRIEF_ENABLED` on the worker role, and on the api role, which gives the owner their brief when they ask for it (below), from `config.briefEnabled`, `false` by default.

The owner sets their brief by asking their assistant, which calls `brief_settings`: `show` reads it; `time` sets the time it is due, `HH:MM` on their wall clock, on the quarter hour, any other refused; `days` sets every day of the week it goes out, one at least; `pause` holds it back until a date after today, on which it goes out again by itself; `stop` holds it back until they resume it; `resume` ends a stop and a pause alike. Every call whose arguments it can read answers the brief as it stands: its time, its days, the end of a pause under way, whether it is stopped, and the zone it follows. What they chose is kept next to their zone, in their own row of `owner_settings` under row-level security, and each pass reads it, so that a change counts from the next minute. The deletion of their assistant erases it with their zone: the next assistant's brief goes out by default, on the deployment's wall clock until a read names their zone again. The tool, to read the brief as to change it, takes the `settings.write_own` right, which a turn an event started never holds, and a turn that comes from others, an event's or a suggestion's, is never offered the tool, even once its owner's yes resumed it: nothing a third party writes moves or stops a brief. The organization agent, which has no brief, is refused. The tool is offered whether the briefs are on or not.

The brief's turn runs on the api role, admitted as a turn is; refused by rate or a full queue, it waits as an event's turn does (see Admission), its lines `brief turn deferred` and `brief turn abandoned` saying why. Refused as the owner's day, or the share of it their assistant spends on its own, is spent, it goes out all the same, laid out by the harness from the same reads with no model call, and its line `brief written` names the refusal, `refused`. Before the model, the harness reads itself, in the owner's name and with the checks of the model's own calls: the day through `list_calendar_events`, from the brief's date for one day, twenty meetings at most; the invitations that wait for the owner's answer through `list_calendar_events` again, from the brief's date for seven days with `needs_action`, a hundred occurrences at most, of which it keeps each invitation once, a series by its first occurrence, numbered from 1 in the order they start, twenty at most; then, in the zone kept for the owner once those reads named one (see Time zone), their unread mail: their inbox, the mailbox of that role among those `list_mailboxes` lists, then its unread emails through `list_emails`, since the instant the owner's last brief read them, or, before any did, since the same time of their wall clock on the last day before that they have a brief on, Friday for a Monday, fifty at most, newest first, of which it leaves out those sent in bulk; then their tasks through `list_my_tasks`, those overdue then those due today, thirty at most together, the overdue ones first; then the shares made to the owner through `list_received_shares`, since the instant their last brief read them, or, before any did, since the instant their mail is read from, thirty days back at most, twenty at most, newest first, each file and folder numbered after the invitations and the meetings the brief numbers, but those of a shared drive, which no other operation of Drive reaches. From the owner's listening journal it reads what had no turn and no brief named yet, the activities their hourly cap held back, that waited past `TURN_EVENT_MAX_DELAY_MS` for admission or that admission refused once the share of the day their assistant spends on its own was spent, those kept for their brief, such as a meeting's new title or an activity that names them for a reason that expects nothing of them, and those their quiet hours hold: twenty at most, the first that arrived, of which it tells apart the answers to the owner's invitations, which their journal keeps for their brief, and the tasks they assigned themselves, kept for their brief as well. It reads only the applications the owner's assistant listens to (see Listened sources): without Calendar, neither the day nor the invitations, nor the activities Calendar published, and a brief that waits for the owner's answer about the day's read (below) goes out without it; without Mail, no mail; without Tasks, no tasks, nor the activities Tasks published; without Drive, no shares. Such a section is said to nobody, not even as not read, and the line `brief application skipped` gives it with `not_listened`. An application that needs a yes the owner has not given, here once their brief no longer asks for its reads (below) or on the token broker, is left out, and nobody is asked, but for the day's read the broker refuses (below): the line `brief application skipped` names its domain, `calendar`, `mail`, `tasks` or `drive`, its section, `calendar`, `invitations`, `mails`, `tasks` or `shares`, and why, `consent` or `delegation`, as it does a catalog without the contract, `unavailable`, a read that failed, `failed`, an answer the harness cannot read, `unreadable`, or mailboxes none of which is the inbox, `no_inbox`; the mail is left out when either of its reads is, and the tasks likewise. One model call follows, with no tool and no history, after the assistant's persona and the present: the assistant is told its owner's day is starting, in the owner's language, and handed what the reads gave as data, fenced: the day, each meeting's times, status, answer and overlaps as the contract computed them, and under `untrusted` what people wrote, its title, location, description and organizer; the invitations, each with its number and whether it is one of a series; the mail, the instant it is read from, `since`, its emails as the contract gives them, in the order the harness lays them out (below), what Mail computed, whether one is flagged, sent in bulk or sent to the owner, `to_me`, included, and under `untrusted` its sender, subject and preview, whether Mail had more, `truncated`, and the addresses of the people the day's meetings name, their organizers but the owner, `participants`; the tasks as the contract gives them; what arrived since the owner's last brief, `since_last_brief`, when anything did, each activity as the `listening_journal` tool shows it to a turn, a meeting with its number, the number of its invitation when the brief numbers it too, the next ones otherwise, a task with its key, and whether the journal had more, `truncated`; the answers to the owner's invitations, `replies`, each as that tool shows it, with the number of its meeting, given as for what arrived since the last brief, the declines first, then the maybes, the other answers, and the acceptances last; the shares, the instant they are read from, `since`, each share as the contract gives it, what Drive computed, whether it is read only or a shared drive, its files and folders, each with its number but in a shared drive, and under `untrusted` each one's name and who shared it, and whether Drive had more, `truncated`; the tasks the owner assigned themselves, `self_assigned`, each as that tool shows it, with its number, after the shares', and its key; and, for a section not read, why, under `not_read`, but for an application whose read the owner took back, which the harness tells them of itself (below), and one their assistant does not listen to. It is asked for those sections in that order, five items at most each, then how many more there are, a section with nothing in it or not handed left out but for the day, when handed, which says in one line that there is no meeting; of the emails, for those flagged first, then those sent to the owner that ask a question, make a request or give a deadline, or that come from someone of the day's meetings, then how many more are unread; of the answers, for the declines and the maybes named first, then the acceptances counted; and, when it showed invitations, emails, tasks or shared files, for one or two examples of what the owner could answer with their numbers, keys or senders. The brief is an ordinary `m.text` message, which notifies, marked `app.twake.assistant.brief` with `{ "date": <date> }`, and marked as a question too when it offers after it, in a line of its own, to listen to an application the owner never chose for (see Listened sources). As it repeats what people wrote, unasked, it mentions nobody (an empty `m.mentions`, whatever `@room` its text holds), and its HTML renders the model's Markdown as the harness quotes the model in its requests: no link, image, heading or HTML of its own, a link showing its text alone. The conversation keeps it as the assistant's answer, which the owner's next turn reads in its history after the intro the assistant was told and, fenced as data, what the brief's numbers, its tasks' keys and its emails name: the `uid` and `recurrence_id` of each invitation, the `id` and sender of each email the model was handed, whichever it showed, in the order the harness lays them out, the `board_id` and `task_id` of each task, and under `since_last_brief` the `uid` and `recurrence_id` of each meeting that arrived since the last brief, by its number, and the `task_id` of each task, with its key, under `replies` the `uid` and `recurrence_id` of each meeting answered, by its number, under `shares` the `id` and type of each file and folder shared with the owner, by its number, and under `self_assigned` the `task_id` of each task the owner assigned themselves, with its key, by its number. "Decline 2" is then an ordinary turn, with its rules. A newer brief takes them out of the earlier one, which the conversation keeps otherwise; the data a brief was written from stays out of the conversation. Its tokens count in the owner's day. Should the model fail or write nothing, the harness lays out the same sections itself, marked too, in the owner's language: the meetings, each one's times as the calendar gives them and its title, and the titles of the meetings it overlaps; the invitations by their numbers, each one's title, day, times or whole day, a series from its first date, and who invites; the unread emails since the day and time they are read from, flagged ones first, then those sent to the owner, then the latest, each by its sender's name, or else address, and its subject, a flagged one said so, and that more are unread when Mail had more, even when all it gave was sent in bulk; the tasks by their keys, each one's title and the day it was due, or the time it is due today; what arrived since the last brief, a meeting by its number and a task by its key, each one's title and what it was, an invitation, a move, a new title, a cancellation, a counter-proposal, a task assigned to them or another activity; the answers to the owner's invitations, each by its meeting's number and title, who answered and what, the declines and the maybes named, then how many accepted; the shares, each file and folder by its number, but in a shared drive, said so, a folder said so too, and who shared it, by their name, or else address, or else as someone; the tasks the owner assigned themselves by their numbers, each one's title and key; five items at most a section, then how many more there are, a section with nothing in it left out, a day without meetings in one line, an application not read in one line, but one whose read the owner took back or that their assistant does not listen to, and two examples at most of what to answer, drawn from the invitations, emails, tasks and shared files it shows; people's text is text, never markup. The line `brief written` says who wrote it, `model` or `template`, how many meetings, invitations, emails, tasks, activities, answers to the owner's invitations (`replies`), shares and tasks the owner assigned themselves (`self_assigned`) it was written from, and the tokens. The instants a brief read the owner's mail and the shares made to them are kept in their own row of `owner_settings`, in the transaction that keeps the brief, for their next brief to read from; a brief that did not read one leaves it as it was. In the same transaction, the activities the brief was handed lose their title and times, and an answer who gave it and what it was: the next brief names them no more, and the `listening_journal` tool tells them by their source, their type and what came of them alone; those the owner's quiet hours held come out of the hold, and wake nobody as they end.

The day's read the token broker refuses for want of the owner's permission for their assistant to act for them, `delegation_missing` or `delegation_expired` (see Consent), is the exception: the brief reads nothing else and gives way to the harness's own request about that permission, the one a turn ends on, with the consent link and the offer to try again, in one message marked as a question, and no model call; the line `brief gave way to a question` names its call. That brief waits for the owner's answer, and the next ones say nothing until the day's read works again, whether the broker still refuses it, the gateway fails it or the harness does not make it: their day is read, without asking again, and the line `brief withheld` names the call and the date the brief waits since. The owner's yes, once they gave the platform their permission, runs the day's read again as it was frozen, under the brief's id, then the brief's other reads, and that day's brief goes out in answer to it, admitted as the morning's brief is, marked with its date, at whatever hour the request still lasts; a yes the broker still refuses gets the request again, which supersedes the one it answered. Their no drops the call as any no does, and the briefs still say nothing until the day's read works again; so does a request left unanswered until it expires. A brief that goes out ends the wait: once the day's read works again, the next morning's brief comes back by itself, asking nothing, and the request it gave way to closes as expired, so that a yes to it runs nothing and is told so. The harness keeps one waiting brief per owner, its date and its call, which the deletion of their assistant erases.

An owner's first brief asks for its reads before it reads anything. Until their brief no longer asks for them, a brief whose owner did not let their assistant read their calendar, their mail or their tasks, at the `read` level each, reads nothing and goes out as the harness's own message, in the owner's language, with no model call, marked as a question: what their brief tells, the days and the time it goes out on and how to change them, then one question for the reads they did not allow, in that order, whose call waits for their answer as any request does, under the brief's id, with the tool, the contract and the domain `brief` and the reads it asks for as its arguments; the line `brief asked for its reads` names its call, the reads and how many times it was asked. The conversation keeps it as the assistant's answer. Their yes, in the room or through the API, allows each of those reads on its own, recorded as given where they gave it, which they take back one at a time as any other, has their assistant listen to their mail, unless they chose otherwise (see Listened sources), and that day's brief goes out in answer to it, marked with its date, at whatever hour the question still lasts, from its day's read on, as any brief; the line `brief reads allowed` names them. Their no stops their brief, as `stop` does, and the harness says so itself, with the words that resume it. Left unanswered, the question goes out once more, on the next day their brief goes out on, then the brief after it stops, as on a no, and says so, asking nothing, its question closing as expired, with the line `brief stopped: its question went unanswered`; a brief of a date that asked already says nothing, its line `brief withheld` naming the question's call. A resume asks again, as the first time. A brief that finds the three reads allowed asks nothing, and their assistant then listens to their mail only once they ask it to; the first brief to go out closes the question, should it still wait, as expired: their briefs ask for their reads no more. From then on, a brief goes without a read the owner took back, its line `brief application skipped` saying `consent`, and the first one to go without it says so after it, in a line of its own, with the words to have it read again, then nothing more of it, until a brief reads it again and the owner takes it back once more. Drive's read, which the question never asks for, is the exception: a brief that goes without it for want of the owner's yes says nothing of it. The harness keeps one question per owner, its date, its call and how many times it was asked, and, in their own row of `owner_settings`, whether their brief still asks for its reads and those it told them they took back; the deletion of their assistant erases them, so that the next assistant's first brief asks again.

A brief nobody reads is of no use: once ten working days, Monday to Friday, went by without the owner being seen in their assistant's room, the one their brief goes to, their brief stops, as `stop` does, and says so, with the words that resume it, asking nothing and reading nothing, with the line `brief stopped: its owner was not seen`. The owner is seen there by any message of theirs, with words or not, and by any public read receipt of theirs (`m.read`), which Synapse pushes to the matrix role with the other ephemeral events: the role keeps the instant it received the last of them, nothing else, in their own row of `owner_settings`. A private receipt (`m.read.private`) is theirs alone: the Matrix specification keeps it from an application service, as Synapse does from 1.162 on, so it counts for nothing, even where an older Synapse still pushes it, and an owner whose client sends only private receipts is seen by their messages alone. Anyone else's receipts there, their assistant's own included, and their words and receipts in any other room, count for nothing. The count goes from the day they were last seen, on their wall clock, to the brief's date, neither of them counted, so that the brief of the tenth working day goes out and the next one stops; before they were ever seen there, it starts from their first brief. The deletion of their assistant erases it, the next assistant's first brief starting the count again.

The owner also has their brief when they ask for it: their assistant calls `brief_now`, which only their own turns are offered, never a turn an event or a suggestion started, and none while `BRIEF_ENABLED` is off; the organization agent is refused. It is the brief of their date, on the wall clock of their zone, whether it is due that day or not and whether it is stopped or paused, which it leaves as it is, as it leaves the days and the time they chose. It reads as a brief does, their mail since their last brief, but tells only the meetings of the day not over yet, the one under way and the all-day ones included. It takes that date's wake-up, under the lock of the owner's wake-ups, and counts among their `WAKEUPS_PER_HOUR`, which never holds it back, so that the brief of that date goes out no more; one a pass took first stays as it is. A question or a request about their permission that their brief waits on closes as expired, and the brief asks what it still needs: the question of its reads, while it asks for them, which counts as one more time asked, or the request about their permission, when the broker refuses the day's read; a yes to either gives the brief of the rest of their day. The brief, or what it asks, is the answer of their turn, which ends there with no other model call: marked as the brief of its date, as the scheduler's is, and kept in the conversation as the call, with what the brief's numbers, its tasks' keys and its emails name as its result, and the brief as the assistant's answer. It takes what the earlier briefs named out of the conversation, as a newer brief does, and the next brief takes out what it named. Its tokens spend the owner's day as their words do, none of the share their assistant spends on its own (see Admission). The line `brief asked for` gives its date.
