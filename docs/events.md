# Events

Events reach the assistants from RabbitMQ alone, which the worker role listens to: the `activity` exchange, where the applications publish their CloudEvents, and Calendar's fanout of the invitations it sends (see below). Each event comes with its turn, in the room of the owner it is for: the model is handed what the event says, and never reads it again through the contracts. Its system prompt tells the model that it may answer nothing at all when nothing in the event is useful to its owner, and otherwise to suggest it in a few words, preparing at most one action: a turn that ends on no words posts nothing in the room and keeps nothing of itself in the conversation. The turn the owner's yes resumes from it, as any turn of theirs, still ends on words, or on the notice of a failed turn when the model writes none. The harness takes no event over HTTP: `POST /v1/events`, through which a dispatcher posted events, is gone, and a deployment that still sets `EVENTS_CLIENT_IDS`, the service clients it took them from, starts as before and ignores it. The chart no longer renders it, and a release that still sets `config.eventsClientIds` renders as before.

## The activity exchange

With `ACTIVITY_ENABLED`, the worker role reads the `activity` exchange of RabbitMQ, where the applications publish their CloudEvents, as ADR 001 and 006 shape them, through `@linagora/rabbitmq-client` as Twake Tasks does. It declares a quorum queue of its own, `<RABBITMQ_PREFIX>.activity`, with a single active consumer, so that events keep their order whatever the replicas, a delivery limit of five, a consumer timeout of a day, at-least-once dead lettering into its own exchange `<RABBITMQ_PREFIX>.dlx` and queue `<RABBITMQ_PREFIX>.activity.dlq`, and reject-publish overflow. The consumer timeout keeps the broker from taking back an event the worker holds while it tries it again, which would count as a delivery, after the half an hour RabbitMQ 3.13 gives by default; it is an argument of the queue, since the instance's user may set no policy, and holds for a queue declared with it. It binds that queue to the types of `ACTIVITY_TYPES` only, and to its own dead letter exchange under its own name, which keeps its dead letters' key the same whatever the types. It never declares the `activity` exchange, and only checks that it is there: its RabbitMQ user may configure and write `^<prefix>\.` and read `^(activity|<prefix>\..+)$`, and nothing more. A queue keeps the arguments it was declared with. A type taken out of `ACTIVITY_TYPES` keeps its binding, which the library never removes, until it is removed on the broker; meanwhile the worker takes its events and drops them. Dead letters moved back into the queue come by its own name, and are read as the other events.

The worker reads one message at a time. Each recipient of the event's `data.recipients`, whatever its reason, is woken when their email, lowercased, is on the mail domain, as for the principals (`MATRIX_MAIL_DOMAIN`, or else `MATRIX_SERVER_NAME`, without which the harness refuses to start listening), when they have an assistant with a room, when their assistant listens to the application the event comes from (see Listened sources), and when they are not the event's actor, by email or by uuid: a task they assigned themselves, or that their assistant assigned them on their yes, as them, wakes them not, and their listening journal keeps it for their brief (see Listening journal), while their other own actions leave nothing. Their assistant gets a turn of origin event in that room, serialized with their other turns: the model is told what arrived, in the owner's language, a task assigned to them or an event of another type, and handed the event as one line of JSON between fences of a random nonce, what the application computed (type, ids, key, link, time, actor, reason) apart from what people wrote, under `untrusted` (title, board name, preview). Whatever that turn prepares to write waits for the owner's yes (see Consent), and its contract calls carry the bare id of the event as their correlation id. A wake-up is kept by the event's source and id and the owner it woke, in the transaction that queues the turn, before the message is acknowledged: a redelivery, a restart or a replay of the dead letter queue wakes nobody twice, and two recipients of one event are each woken once. The `wakeups` table holds identifiers and dates only, and nothing of a recipient without an assistant. Each recipient is read on its own: one with a malformed email, uuid or reason is left out, and logged by its place and the names of its faulty fields, never their values, and past the first hundred the others are left out with a log line that counts them. Text other people wrote is cut rather than refused, at 1,000 characters for a title or a preview and 200 for a board's name, and an optional field the application got wrong, such as a link that is no http or https URL, is left out and named in a log line. Only a message that is no CloudEvent with an id, a source, a type and its `data.object` goes to the dead letter queue, at once.

An event that fails holds back none of those behind it, and is never lost. A transient failure, the database out of reach or a connection lost, is tried again for as long as it lasts, the wait doubling from a second up to a minute, and never sends the event to the dead letter queue; any other failure is tried five times in all, with the same waits, then the event goes there and the next ones go on. A message that brings the worker down whenever it holds it goes there too: at the last delivery the queue allows, after it came back five times, as the broker counts them in `x-delivery-count`, the worker dead-letters it itself, rather than leave it to the broker with no line to say so. Once its cause is fixed, the dead letter queue is replayed by moving its messages back into the queue, with a shovel or the management UI: a replayed event wakes nobody it already woke within the retention, and a dead letter replayed after it, older than `WAKEUPS_RETENTION_MS`, may wake its owners again. Each message gives one JSON line once the worker is done with it, `event handled`: the event's source, id (`eventId`) and type, how many recipients it names, its `outcome`, and under `outcomes` how many of its recipients had each, `woken`, `capped`, `quiet_hours`, `for_brief`, `duplicate`, `unlistened`, `no_assistant`, `ignored`, or `invalid` for one it cannot read. The outcome of the message is the first of these one of its recipients had, or `ignored` for an event of a type it no longer lists, or `dead_lettered`, with the `reason`: `not JSON`, the attribute at fault, such as `no source` or `no data.object`, `failed 5 times`, or `delivery_limit`. Each failed attempt gives an `event failed` line, which says whether the failure is `transient`. No line carries what an event says, at any level: the library's lines go out without the bodies and the parse errors they would quote, and those the worker's own lines restate, at debug.

A broker out of reach, one that never answers, or an `activity` exchange missing never stops the role: the worker serves its health at once and listens in the background, and tries again without end, the waits doubling up to a minute, each failed attempt logged as `listen failed`, its connection closed. Each connection to the broker has ten seconds to open, or the milliseconds of `connection_timeout` in the query of `ACTIVITY_AMQP_URL`, as RabbitMQ's URI spec names it: a broker behind a firewall that drops packets would otherwise hold it for the minutes the system takes to give up. Once it listens, the library connects again by itself when the broker goes and comes back, and reads the queue again; when it cannot, the worker starts over as at its start. The worker's health check says whether the listener reads its queue, `"activity": "connected"` or `"disconnected"`, and answers 200 either way, so that a broker down never restarts the role. A known limit: the `activity` exchange deleted and declared again while the worker listens takes the queue's bindings with it, and the worker, still connected, says so, while no event reaches its queue; it binds the queue again when it connects again, so a restart of the worker role, after such a change on the broker, brings the events back. The worker forgets the wake-ups older than `WAKEUPS_RETENTION_MS`, 30 days by default, at its start and every hour, and logs how many went: within the retention, a redelivery or a replay wakes nobody twice, and after it the event is a new one.

An owner is woken at most `WAKEUPS_PER_HOUR` times in a rolling hour, 20 by default, whatever the source, counted in the `wakeups` table, so that a burst, a mass assignment or what piled up during an outage, drowns neither their room nor their quota: past it, an event wakes them no more, is taken from the queue all the same, is logged as `event capped`, and their listening journal notes it `capped` (see Listening journal), which keeps it from waking them later. A redelivery stays a duplicate, whatever the count. The chart renders `config.wakeupsPerHour` as `WAKEUPS_PER_HOUR` on the worker role, only when set.

The chart renders these settings on the worker role only: `config.activityEnabled`, the URL from `config.activityAmqpUrlSecret` and `config.activityAmqpUrlSecretKey`, by default `ACTIVITY_AMQP_URL` in the chart's own Secret, `config.activityTypes` and `config.rabbitmqPrefix`, and `config.wakeupsRetentionMs`, whether the worker listens or not; it refuses a listening worker without `config.matrixServerName` or `config.matrixMailDomain`, which every role gets when set. With `networkPolicy.rabbitmqNamespace`, it lets that role alone out to the broker's namespace, on `networkPolicy.rabbitmqPort` (5672). The suite runs a RabbitMQ 3.13 container, as on dev, from `RABBITMQ_IMAGE` when set.

## Calendar's invitations

With `CALENDAR_ENABLED`, the worker role also reads Calendar's own fanout, `calendar:event:notificationEmail:send`, on the vhost `CALENDAR_AMQP_URL` names, `calendar` on the platform, where Calendar's side service sends a notification for each invitee of each change to a meeting: the invitations of personal calendars never reach the activity exchange. It reads it on a connection of its own, through a quorum queue of its own, `<RABBITMQ_PREFIX>.calendar`, which keeps the guarantees of the activity queue: one message at a time, a single active consumer, a delivery limit of five, a consumer timeout of a day, and at-least-once dead lettering into `<RABBITMQ_PREFIX>.dlx` on that vhost and `<RABBITMQ_PREFIX>.calendar.dlq`. It never declares the fanout: there too, its RabbitMQ user may configure and write `^<prefix>\.`, and read `^(calendar:event:notificationEmail:send|<prefix>\..+)$`.

The fanout carries every tenant's invitations: a notification for an invitee off the mail domain, or that names none, is foreign to the instance, taken without effect before anything else of it is read, and logged nowhere, even one that cannot be read, so that another tenant's invitations never reach its dead letter queue. Of the others, a new invitation wakes its invitee, `method` REQUEST in any case with `isNewEvent` true, and so does a move of a meeting they were invited to, a REQUEST without it whose `changes` name `dtstart` or `dtend`; one whose `changes` name the title alone, `summary`, is kept for their brief with no turn (see Listening journal). The cancellation of a meeting they were invited to, of one occurrence of its series or of the whole series, `method` CANCEL, wakes them too, whatever they answered it. An invitee's counter-proposal of another time for a meeting, `method` COUNTER, wakes the organizer it is for, and an invitee's answer, `method` REPLY, is kept for the organizer's brief with no turn, either only when its VEVENT's ORGANIZER names the person it is for: else it is taken without effect, its line saying `ignored` and `not the organizer`. `changes` loses `location` and `description` before anything else of it is read, and of the title only that it changed is read. A change to a meeting whose VEVENT gives the invitee's ATTENDEE the PARTSTAT DECLINED, or any other update, is taken without effect, its line saying `ignored`; that VEVENT is the meeting as the change left it, the one before coming with a counter-proposal alone, so that an invitee whose answer the organizer's client set back to NEEDS-ACTION as it moved the meeting is woken as one who never answered. The free text the harness never reads, DESCRIPTION, LOCATION, COMMENT, ATTACH and X-ALT-DESC, is taken out of the iCalendar first, with the lines folded onto it, so that none written wrong can set the invitation aside. ical.js then reads the rest, folded lines, escaped text, time zones, all-day dates and times in UTC included, and the invitation is its first VEVENT that is no occurrence, else its first. Its invitee, `recipientEmail` trimmed and lowercased, is woken as a recipient of the activity exchange is, for the reason `invited`, and its actor is its organizer, its ORGANIZER without `mailto:`, else `senderEmail`. The organizer a counter-proposal or an answer is for, its `recipientEmail` too, takes it for the reason `organizer`, a counter-proposal alone waking them, and its actor is the invitee, `senderEmail` trimmed and lowercased. The wake-up has the type `com.twake.calendar.event.invited.v1`, `com.twake.calendar.event.moved.v1` for a move, `com.twake.calendar.event.renamed.v1` for a new title, `com.twake.calendar.event.cancelled.v1` for a cancellation, `com.twake.calendar.event.countered.v1` for a counter-proposal or `com.twake.calendar.event.replied.v1` for an answer, the source `twake://calendar`, and the id the calendar producer gave the invitation, which the gateway's audit records carry as the turn's correlation id: the hex SHA-256 of its UID exactly as written, the person it is for, its SEQUENCE, 0 when it has none, and its RECURRENCE-ID exactly as written when it has one, joined with `|`, a cancellation adding `CANCEL` after them, a counter-proposal `COUNTER`, the invitee, and the DTSTART and the DTEND, or else the DURATION, it proposes exactly as written, and an answer `REPLY`, the invitee and the answer, so that a redelivery wakes nobody twice, none of them is taken for the invitation at its SEQUENCE, and each time an invitee proposes, and each answer they give, counts once. The model is handed the start, the end and the occurrence, as RFC 3339 times with the offset of their zone, the zone the calendar defines or else the IANA zone of that name, or as dates for an all-day event, the end being the start plus the DURATION when there is no DTEND, for a move the times it moved from, `previous_start` and `previous_end`, which `changes` gives as wall times in the zone it names, and the organizer's address; for a counter-proposal, in place of the start and the end, the time it proposes, `proposed_start` and `proposed_end`, and the invitee's address; apart from them, under `untrusted`, what people wrote, the organizer or, in a counter-proposal or an answer, the invitee's client: the title cut at 1,000 characters, the UID at 255 and the zone at 64. A field the calendar wrote wrong is left out on its own, and named in an `event fields left out` line, never with what it held; a notification without an iCalendar, a VEVENT or a UID the harness can read goes to the dead letter queue at once, its line saying why, and so do a counter-proposal or an answer whose `senderEmail` is no email address, an answer whose invitee's ATTENDEE gives none of the answers iCalendar names as its PARTSTAT, ACCEPTED, DECLINED, TENTATIVE, DELEGATED or NEEDS-ACTION, and one that is no JSON.

Before the model speaks, the harness asks `read_freebusy` about that slot, an all-day one from midnight to midnight in the owner's zone, the deployment's when none is kept (see Time zone), in the owner's name, with the invitation's exact UID left out, and hands the model the answer after the invitation, fenced as data; without a free/busy contract in the catalog, or for a slot it cannot ask about, such as one without an end or in a zone nobody knows, it says why it did not check, never naming the zone. No log line holds a UID or a zone. The first read of the owner's calendar waits for their yes, as any first read does. The model tells the owner who invites them, to what, when, and whether they are free, and prepares `accept_invitation` with the invitation's UID, which waits for the owner's yes (see Consent). The turn of a move says whether it moved a meeting, one occurrence of a series or the whole series, and checks the new slot, a series' from its DTSTART as an invitation's: the model tells the owner who moved which meeting, from when to when, and whether they are free over its new slot, and prepares `decline_invitation` when that slot conflicts, else `accept_invitation`, for a whole series as for a meeting, since only the owner's yes to the harness's question about a series sets `series` (see Consent). It is offered `read_freebusy` and those two answers alone, and no tool for one occurrence of a series, which the answers cannot reach apart from the rest of it: the model tells the owner they answer it in Calendar. The turn the owner's yes resumes is told of no event and keeps the tools of any event's turn. A move whose turn prepares an answer while the owner's yes to the meeting's former one is still awaited replaces that question, as any newer request does, so that the answer prepared for the former slot is never sent. The turn of a cancellation says whether it cancelled a meeting, one occurrence of a series or the whole series, checks nothing and is offered no tool: the model tells the owner in one sentence who cancelled which meeting and when it was to take place, and asks them nothing. The turn of a counter-proposal says whether it is about a meeting, one occurrence of a series or the whole series, and checks the time proposed as an invitation's slot, the meeting left out: the model tells the owner which invitee proposes which time for which meeting and whether they are free then, is offered `read_freebusy` alone, whatever the proposal is about, and prepares nothing, as the owner changes a meeting's time in Calendar. The listener copes with failures as the activity one does (see The activity exchange): a transient failure is tried again without end, any other five times before the dead letter queue, a notification back at its delivery limit is dead-lettered with its line, each one gives its `event handled` line, under the source `twake://calendar` and the id of its invitation, and no line carries what it says. A broker out of reach or a fanout missing never stops the role, `connection_timeout` in the query of `CALENDAR_AMQP_URL` sets how long a connection has to open, and a fanout declared again while the worker listens has the known limit of the activity exchange. The worker's health check says `"calendar": "connected"` or `"disconnected"`. The chart renders these settings on the worker role only: `config.calendarEnabled`, and the URL from `config.calendarAmqpUrlSecret` and `config.calendarAmqpUrlSecretKey`, by default `CALENDAR_AMQP_URL` in the chart's own Secret; the egress rule to the broker serves both sources.

## Listening journal

Each owner's listening journal, under their own principal, notes the activities a source published for them that wake their assistant, that their hourly cap holds back, that their quiet hours hold, or that it keeps for their brief, from the activity exchange or Calendar's fanout: an activity's source, type and id, when it arrived on the worker's clock, what came of it, and what identifies and shows it. Of an object of the activity exchange, its type, id and key, and the title of a task or a meeting alone, never what a message or a file is called; of a meeting, its UID and RECURRENCE-ID, its title and its times, the times a move moved it from, the time an invitee proposes and their address, or an invitee's address and answer, never its place, its description or a comment. What came of it is `woken` until the turn it woke ends, then `suggested` once that turn answered, `nothing_useful` once it ended on no words, `abandoned` once it waited past `TURN_EVENT_MAX_DELAY_MS` for admission, `share_spent` once admission refused it as the owner's day, or the share of it their assistant spends on its own, was spent (see Admission), `failed` otherwise; `capped` when the owner's hourly cap held it back, or `for_brief` for a task they assigned themselves, a meeting's new title alone or an invitee's answer to a meeting they organize, which call for no word at once, both with no turn; `quiet_hours` while their quiet hours hold it, then `woken` once it wakes them as they end (see Quiet hours). An activity their journal noted wakes the owner no more, woken or not; a brief leaves no row. The owner's own turns have the `listening_journal` tool, which lists the activities received since midnight in their zone, the deployment's (`ASSISTANT_TIMEZONE`) until a read named one (see Time zone), with what came of each and their titles under `untrusted`; a turn an activity woke never has it. The worker role erases an activity's title and times after seven days, as the brief that names an activity does (see Morning brief), after which the tool gives its source, its type and what came of it alone, and deletes it after `WAKEUPS_RETENTION_MS`, at its start and every hour, with a `listening journal purged` line that counts both. Each activity gives one `activity noted` line at info once settled, from the worker for one capped, held for the quiet hours or kept for the brief and from the api role once the turn it woke ended: its source, id, type, owner and outcome, never what it says.

## Listened sources

Each owner chooses the applications their assistant listens to: Calendar and Tasks by default, Mail from their yes to the question of their first brief (see Morning brief), and Drive once they ask, unless they chose otherwise. An activity, from the activity exchange or Calendar's fanout, is the application's by the source it was published under: `twake://calendar` is Calendar's, `twake://tasks` is Tasks', and any other source, Mail, Drive and Chat included, publishes for nobody until the harness knows its producer, with an `activity of an unknown source, listened to by nobody` warning that names it. An activity of an application the owner's assistant does not listen to wakes nothing and leaves nothing, in their room or their journal, and takes none of their wake-ups: its recipient's outcome is `unlistened`. A brief is no activity. Mail and Drive publish nothing the harness takes: listening there wakes nothing, and only has the owner's brief read their mail, or the shares made to them, as their brief reads only the applications their assistant listens to (see Morning brief). The owner's own turns have three tools: `listened_sources` lists the applications their assistant listens to, those it could listen to but does not, and those it cannot listen to yet; `stop_listening_to_source` stops listening to Calendar, Tasks, Mail or Drive, which their assistant still reads when they ask; `listen_to_source` starts again. Listening to an application whose data the owner has not let their assistant read asks them first, with the question of a first read there, which shows the call: their yes lets it read there and starts listening, as any first read's yes allows the application from then on. Each choice ends the turn on the harness's own words, in the owner's language, which for Mail and Drive say that their brief alone tells of them, and gives a `listened source set` line with the source and whether it is listened to. The choices take the `settings.write_own` right, and a turn an activity woke has none of these tools. Migration 0092 has the assistant of every owner who had let it read their mail listen there, unless they had chosen for Mail already: their brief does not change.

## Quiet hours

Each owner has quiet hours, during which their assistant posts nothing on its own: a daily range, on the quarter hour, which may cross midnight, and whole days, on the wall clock of their calendar's zone, the deployment's (`ASSISTANT_TIMEZONE`) until a read named one (see Time zone), so that they keep their times there when the clocks change. Unless they chose others, they are the deployment's, `QUIET_HOURS_DEFAULT`, from 20:00 to 08:00 and all of Saturday and Sunday by default. They hold back what the assistant does on its own alone: an owner who writes at night is answered, and their brief keeps its time. An activity that would wake the owner during their quiet hours, once their own actions and what is kept for their brief are set aside, is held instead, `quiet_hours` in their listening journal with an `activity noted` line, until it comes out of them: at their next brief, when it goes out on the day the quiet hours end (`BRIEF_ENABLED` on, a day of their brief, neither paused nor stopped), or else at their end. One about a meeting that starts before then, at its time, at the one it had for a move or at the one a counter-proposal proposes, wakes them at once. Their brief names what was held under `since_last_brief`, as it does what had no turn, and ends its hold in the transaction that keeps it (see Morning brief). At each pass, briefs on or off, the worker role's scheduler wakes the owner's assistant for what was held once its time came, the end of the quiet hours, or three hours past the brief's time for an activity that brief was to name, should it never go out: the first due first, as an event's turn, `woken` then in their journal, within their `WAKEUPS_PER_HOUR`, the rest at the next passes, with an `event queued` line each. A held activity keeps the wake-up its turn will need, as its listener cleaned it on arrival, in the owner's own rows of `held_activities` under row-level security, until a brief names it or it wakes them; one of an application they no longer listen to, or for an assistant no longer in its room, comes out of the hold and wakes nobody. The assistant's notice of a spent share is posted out of the quiet hours alone: a refusal during them posts nothing, and the first one past them posts it (see Admission).

The owner sets their quiet hours by asking their assistant, which calls `quiet_hours`: `show` reads them; `hours` sets the daily range, `start` and `end` as `HH:MM` on their wall clock, on the quarter hour, one of them kept as it stands when they name the other alone, the same time for both refused; `days` sets every whole quiet day of the week, none when empty; `none` removes both. Every call whose arguments it can read answers the quiet hours as they stand, their range, their days and the zone they follow, and every call but `show` that it accepts ends the turn on the harness's own words, which say them in the owner's language, or that there are none. What they chose is kept next to their brief, in their own row of `owner_settings`, and read at each activity. As `brief_settings` does, the tool takes the `settings.write_own` right, and a turn that comes from others, an event's or a suggestion's, is never offered it: nothing a third party writes silences or wakes the owner. The organization agent is refused. The harness refuses to start with a `QUIET_HOURS_DEFAULT` it cannot read: one range at most, `HH:MM-HH:MM` on the quarter hour, the same time for both refused, the days of the week by their English names, or `none`, which an empty value also means. The chart does not set it.
